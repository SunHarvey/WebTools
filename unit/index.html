Convert common metric, imperial and data units

This converter covers a defined set of everyday measurements rather than an open-ended unit catalog. It has exactly six categories: length, mass, temperature, area, volume and data size. Select a category first, then choose two units that belong to it. The controls calculate locally as you edit and let you swap the source and target without retyping the value.

How to perform a unit conversion

  1. Select one measurement categoryChoose length, mass, temperature, area, volume or data size. The source and target menus are rebuilt with the implementation-defined unit set for that category.
  2. Enter a value and two unitsType a finite number, then pick the source and target. Conversion happens immediately when the value or a menu changes; the Convert button runs the same calculation explicitly.
  3. Read or reverse the conversionUse the displayed result with its unit symbol, or choose Swap to exchange source and target. Keep the category and unit convention with any value you copy into another document.

Representative conversion examples

Metric and imperial length

Convert 5 kilometers to miles for a route note, or 12 inches to centimeters for a product dimension. Length options include meter, kilometer, centimeter, millimeter, mile, yard, foot, inch and nautical mile; each is reduced through the category's meter scale.

Temperature with an offset

Convert 68 °F to 20 °C, or 0 °C to 273.15 K. Temperature is not handled by multiplying a scale alone: the implementation uses an affine temperature conversion with a scale and offset for Celsius, Fahrenheit and kelvin.

Storage labels that use different bases

Compare 1 MB with MiB when reading disk, network or memory documentation. Decimal kB and MB are based on powers of 1000, while binary KiB and MiB use powers of 1024. GB, TB, GiB and TiB follow the same distinction.

US recipe and container volume

Translate a US gallon into liters, or a US cup into milliliters. The included customary choices are US gallon, US quart, US cup and US fluid ounce. They are explicitly US definitions, so similarly named imperial volume measures are not interchangeable.

Precision, scope and limitations

  • Units can only be converted within the currently selected category. The tool cannot convert across categories, such as length to area, even if a real-world calculation could relate them with additional dimensions.
  • Arithmetic uses JavaScript Number and therefore IEEE-754 floating-point behavior. The conversion function reduces residue with toPrecision(15). The visible formatter prints safe integers directly and applies toPrecision(12) to all other finite values. Those choices improve readability but do not create arbitrary precision.
  • The converter applies stored scale and offset definitions without uncertainty, significant-figure or measurement-context modeling. Very large or very small inputs can expose Number limits, and displayed digits should not be treated as laboratory accuracy.
  • There is no physical plausibility validation. For example, the temperature category accepts values below absolute zero because it performs the mathematical affine conversion rather than enforcing a physics rule.
  • The available categories do not include currency, speed or time. Volume uses US customary entries only, and the exact unit menus shown in the interface define this tool's coverage.

Unit conversion questions

What is the difference between MB and MiB?

One MB is 1,000,000 bytes, while one MiB is 1,048,576 bytes. The decimal prefixes kB, MB, GB and TB advance by 1000; the binary prefixes KiB, MiB, GiB and TiB advance by 1024. Choose the convention used by the specification you are reading.

Are gallons and cups imperial or US units?

The volume menu labels gallon, quart, cup and fluid ounce as US units and uses US conversion factors. Do not use those results as UK imperial quantities, because the names can be similar while the defined volumes differ.

Why can the result have many digits?

Some conversion ratios do not end neatly in decimal notation. The tool keeps useful significant digits and removes common floating-point residue, but the final number remains an approximation represented by JavaScript Number. Round it further according to your real measurement precision.

Can I convert a negative or scientific-notation value?

Yes, when the trimmed input is nonblank and JavaScript converts it to a finite Number. This includes many negative and scientific-notation values. The tool does not judge whether that input is meaningful for the selected measurement, so context-specific validation remains your responsibility.
